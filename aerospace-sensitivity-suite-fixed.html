NTSB/AAR-89/03 · April 28 1988 · Boeing 737-200 N73711
Aloha Airlines Flight 243 — Fuselage Structural Failure at 24,000 ft
What happened: After 89,680 pressurisation cycles, fatigue cracks at lap joint stringer S-10L linked up. The upper fuselage skin separated — approximately 18 feet torn away. The failure initiated exactly where the structural margin of safety had silently reached zero.

The sensitivity connection: Small changes in crack density, corrosion rate, and inspection threshold drove enormous changes in residual structural strength — the real-world equivalent of structural sensitivity analysis.

NASA LANGLEY · NTRS-20100016275 · Aerostructures Test Wing
NASA Langley Flutter Sensitivity — Damping Uncertainty Study
The NASA finding: Damping identification errors of just 3–5% in ground vibration testing shift flutter speed predictions by 3–5% (FRVF method) or up to 5% (Loewner Framework). In a fighter aircraft flying at 400 m/s, a 5% error means the actual flutter boundary is 20 m/s closer than the pilot believes.

FAA / USAF STABILITY DATA · F-16 CG Sensitivity Study
F-16 Longitudinal Stability — CG Range and Derivative Sensitivity
The design challenge: The F-16 was intentionally designed with a slightly unstable longitudinal static margin (SM ≈ −2% to −5%) relying entirely on fly-by-wire. Any failure in stability augmentation immediately renders the aircraft uncontrollable. A CG shift of just 2–3% chord aft of the allowable limit causes the aircraft to depart controlled flight.
